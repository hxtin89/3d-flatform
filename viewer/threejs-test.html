Optimizations

Design

Basemap
Allowed error in screen pixels. 1 = pixel-perfect and expensive, higher = softer and far fewer tiles (measured: 23 tiles at 1, 16 at 2, 9 at 4). The point cloud itself runs at 8
Ground fog
Off removes the effect from the shader entirely rather than turning it down, so it costs nothing at all — use it to read off what it is worth in fps
Past 100% the fog saturates earlier instead of overshooting its colour — the coverage itself is capped at 100%
Falloff upward from the base (1/e height)
0 = fog reaches the ground at full density · higher = a band hanging in the canopy with clear air beneath
1 = physical (Beer-Lambert) · <1 fog starts early · >1 the near field stays clear and density moves into the distance
Smaller = denser. e-folding distance along the fog base, not a cutoff. 0 = full coverage immediately
0 = follows the daylight automatically · 1 = a fixed colour around the clock
Ground under the point cloud
Off removes the effect from the shader entirely rather than turning it down, so it costs nothing at all — use it to read off what it is worth in fps Replaces the satellite imagery with a solid colour where point data exists — the shape follows a coverage mask splatted from the points themselves, so gaps in the survey — the river, the missing strip — keep showing the map
Paints the coverage mask in flat magenta at full strength, so every pixel the mask claims is unmistakable — the fastest way to tell a mask fault from a cloud shadow or a dark patch of imagery
0% = the basemap at its own brightness below · 100% = the flat colour · in between blends the two
No daylight grading, no ground fog, no vignette — those are applied before the patch. Distance fog is not: three applies it after, so at range the colour is tinted toward the haze
Independent of the global basemap brightness, and free of fog and vignette
100% = imagery fully replaced · below that the terrain shows through
Radius in metres the coverage is averaged over. Smooths the outline and gives the threshold room to move the edge. Too wide and it stops seeing gaps — past about half the river's width the patch closes back over it
Where to cut the blurred coverage. 50% sits on the outline, higher pulls the edge inward, lower pushes it out
Distance fog
Off removes the effect from the shader entirely rather than turning it down, so it costs nothing at all — use it to read off what it is worth in fps
Fraction of the view distance where the haze starts. Small = haze begins right at the camera
Fraction of the view distance where everything turns sky-coloured — this hides the map edge. Always kept above the onset
Both are fractions of the current view distance, not metres — so the haze keeps the same proportions over the canopy as it does 90 km out. Colour follows the daylight.

On = the focal plane follows the point at screen centre · Off = a fixed distance from the camera
With autofocus an offset from the aimed point (negative pulls focus nearer), otherwise the absolute distance
Distance past the focal plane to full blur. Small = shallow and cinematic, large = almost everything sharp
